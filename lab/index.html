3D Lab

Models built automatically, then shown as they are

The 3D Lab is where a structure becomes a model: from drawings, from a laser scan, or both. The same model then drives a live twin you can fly through, a photoreal turntable and ray-traced films.

Automated modelling

Two ways into 3D, neither drawn by hand

From drawings

Vector PDF drawings to a member-level model

The lines, dimension chains and labels of a CAD drawing set are read directly. Against an independent model of the same tower, 90% of members matched and 97% of nodes lay within 150 mm.

How it worksSee the result
From point clouds

Laser scans to geometry and analysis

Components are recognised in the point cloud and fitted with parametric geometry, then handed to structural analysis. On a real bridge the method reached 91% Intersection over Union against critical outlines.

How it works

Drawing figures from one real 66 m tower with anonymised geometry; independent engineering review is in progress. Point-cloud figures from the founder's published work.

Live twin

Turn it, click it, fly along it

Double-circuit lattice steel tower, 340 m spans. The tower, conductors and terrain are procedurally generated illustrative models, not design drawings. Utilisation colours are synthetic demonstration data (evidence level E1), not calculation results. Open full screen.

Tab 1

Live twin

AIoT

Then the sensors go onto the model

Each gauge is registered on the member it sits on, its records are checked and corrected, and the twin is calibrated against measurement and validated on an event it has not seen.

AIoT and sensors

Why it matters

Results people can see, and data machines can learn from

Owners and reviewers understand a structure faster when they can turn it and click it. The same pipeline also writes depth, segmentation and camera layers for every frame: physically consistent synthetic data for training vision and world models.
